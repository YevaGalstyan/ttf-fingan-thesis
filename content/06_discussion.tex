% # Ch5 — Fu et al. (2019) as external support for the distributional failure

% **Source:** Fu, Chen, Zeng, Zhuang, Sudjianto (2019), *Time Series Simulation by
% Conditional Generative Adversarial Net*, arXiv:1904.11419. Wells Fargo Corporate
% Model Risk. Preprint, not peer-reviewed — use as corroboration, not primary
% authority.

% **Why it matters:** gives two mechanisms for thin tails and absent volatility
% clustering that are *independent of FinGAN's loss function*. Shifts the
% contribution from "the economics-driven terms do not transfer to TTF" toward
% "the distributional failure has architectural and sample-size roots that no loss
% combination was going to fix." Consistent with the ablation result that no loss
% combination reliably beats ForGAN.

% ---

% ## Mechanism 1 — Small samples break higher moments

% **Their finding.** CGAN trained on a 2-dim VAR(1) with Student-t noise (DF=20).
% At n = 1,000 the generated excess kurtosis was systematically underestimated,
% in some cases falling *below* the true value — tails too thin. Repeated at
% n = 20,000: kurtosis matched. Their stated conclusion is that CGAN training is
% affected by training sample size, especially for capturing higher-order moments.

% **Link to our results.** Training set is a fraction of 3,998 trading days.
% Two identical l=10 runs gave excess kurtosis −0.17 and 0.60 — near zero,
% occasionally negative. Same failure mode, same direction.

% **How to state it.** As consistent supporting evidence, not proof. Their setup
% is a synthetic bivariate VAR with a one-lag condition; ours is a univariate
% commodity return series with a ten-lag condition. The mechanism transfers, the
% numbers do not.

% **Careful:** do not let this collapse into "we just needed more data." TTF has
% existed since 2010 and the sample is what the market provides. The honest claim
% is that the data requirement of the method exceeds what this market can supply —
% which is itself a finding about applying FinGAN to commodities.

% ---

% ## Mechanism 2 — The condition variable may be the wrong one

% **The argument.** GARCH-type series have weak dependence in the mean and strong
% dependence in the variance. Fu et al. observe that conditioning on past *levels*
% feeds the network the quantity carrying little information, while the
% informative quantity — recent volatility — must be inferred. They tested
% conditioning directly on sigma_t^2 against conditioning on sigma_{t-1}^2 and
% found the former clearly better, and concluded that the choice of condition
% matters.

% **Link to our results.** Our condition is ten past log returns — levels, not
% volatility. Gives a mechanistic reason for weak volatility clustering that does
% not depend on the loss function at all.

% **Also explains the l=30 result.** Lengthening the window introduced some
% clustering and increased kurtosis, but worsened scale (std dropped). Under this
% reading, l=30 helps because a longer window makes recent volatility easier to
% infer — not because more history is better per se. Consistent with the earlier
% finding that l=30 is a trade-off rather than a uniform improvement.

% **Narrative hinge to 2.5.3.** Fu et al. list, as future work, adding LSTM layers
% to the generator so it can extract historical volatility automatically. That is
% what ForGAN already does. So the LSTM is not incidental architecture — it is the
% component expected to recover the volatility structure that raw level
% conditioning leaves implicit. Our results suggest it does not do so
% sufficiently on TTF. Worth stating explicitly: this is the mechanism the
% architecture was supposed to provide, and it underdelivered.

% ---

%   to the condition alongside the returns.
% - Compare volatility clustering and kurtosis against the l=10 baseline.
% - Multi-seed, mean ± std — single runs are not defensible.

% **Both outcomes are useful.**
% - Clustering appears → mechanism demonstrated rather than asserted. Strongest
%   version of the Ch5 argument.
% - Clustering does not appear → the limitation is deeper than condition design,
%   strengthening the architectural reading. Also a clean statement of what would
%   need to change for the method to work on this market.

% **Priority:** below the parametric benchmarks and the market option pricing
% benchmark. Nice-to-have. If time runs short, the argument can be made from
% Fu et al. plus the existing l=10 / l=30 comparison without running it — state
% it as a proposed test in future work.

% 1. What failed (from Ch4): fat tails, clustering, skew.
% 2. Why — three mechanisms:
%    a. Loss-term tension: MSE and SR are variance-shrinking by construction;
%       only the adversarial term targets distribution matching. Vuletić et al.
%       observed narrow distributions from these terms themselves.
%    b. Sample size limits on higher moments (Fu et al.).
%    c. Condition carries level information, not volatility information

% 3. Consequence: thin tails → too little mass far from the money → systematic
%    OTM underpricing.

% Mechanisms (a) and (c) are the strongest because they are structural and
% supported by both papers. (b) is real but weakest, since it invites the "get
% more data" response.
